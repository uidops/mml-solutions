# Exercise 2.3 — Unit Upper Triangular Group

> [!question] Problem
> **2.3** Consider the set $\mathcal{G}$ of $3 \times 3$ matrices defined as follows:
>
> $$\mathcal{G} = \left\{ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & x & z \\ 0 & 1 & y \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3} \mid x, y, z \in \mathbb{R} \right\}$$
>
> We define $\cdot$ as the standard matrix multiplication.
>
> Is $(\mathcal{G}, \cdot)$ a group? If yes, is it Abelian? Justify your answer.

## Solution

> [!success] (a) — $(\mathcal{G}, \cdot)$ is a group
>
> **Closure:** For two matrices in $\mathcal{G}$,
> $$\begin{bmatrix} 1 & x & z \\ 0 & 1 & y \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \cdot  \begin{bmatrix} 1 & x' & z' \\ 0 & 1 & y' \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & x + x' & z' + xy' + z\\0 & 1 & y + y'\\0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$
> and $x + x'$, $y + y'$, $x y' + z + z'$ are again real, since $\mathbb{R}$ is closed under standard addition and multiplication. Hence the product is again a unit upper triangular $3 \times 3$ matrix with real parameters, i.e. it lies in $\mathcal{G}$, so $\mathcal{G}$ is closed under $\cdot$. $\blacksquare$
>
> **Associativity:** For three matrices in $\mathcal{G}$,
> $$ \left( \begin{bmatrix} 1 & x & z \\ 0 & 1 & y \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \cdot \begin{bmatrix} 1 & x' & z' \\ 0 & 1 & y' \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}\right) \cdot \begin{bmatrix} 1 & x'' & z'' \\ 0 & 1 & y'' \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & x + x' & z' + xy' + z\\0 & 1 & y + y'\\0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \cdot \begin{bmatrix} 1 & x'' & z'' \\ 0 & 1 & y'' \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} $$
> $$ = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & (x + x') + x'' & z'' + (x+x')y'' + (xy' + z + z')  \\ 0 & 1 & (y + y') + y'' \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$
> $$ = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & x + x' + x'' & x y' + x y'' + x' y'' + z + z' + z'' \\ 0 & 1 & y + y' + y'' \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$
> $$ = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & x + (x' + x'') & (z'' + x'y'' + z') + x(y' + y'') + z \\ 0 & 1 & y + (y' + y'')\\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$
> $$ = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & x & z \\ 0 & 1 & y \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \cdot \begin{bmatrix} 1 & x' + x'' & z'' + x'y'' + z' \\ 0 & 1 & y' + y'' \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$
> $$ =  \begin{bmatrix} 1 & x & z \\ 0 & 1 & y \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \cdot \left( \begin{bmatrix} 1 & x' & z' \\ 0 & 1 & y' \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \cdot \begin{bmatrix} 1 & x'' & z'' \\ 0 & 1 & y'' \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \right)$$
>
> for all three matrices, since both iterated products equal the same matrix with parameters $x + x' + x''$, $y + y' + y''$ and $xy' + x y'' + x' y'' + z + z' + z''$. Therefore $(A \cdot B) \cdot C = A \cdot (B \cdot C)$ on $\mathcal{G}$. $\blacksquare$
>
> **Identity:** The element
>
> $$e = I_3 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \in \mathcal{G}$$
>
> satisfies, for all matrices in $\mathcal{G}$,
>
> $$\begin{bmatrix} 1 & x & z \\ 0 & 1 & y \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \cdot \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & x & z \\ 0 & 1 & y \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$
>
> $$\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \cdot \begin{bmatrix} 1 & x & z \\ 0 & 1 & y \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & x & z \\ 0 & 1 & y \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix},$$
>
> using the product rule computed in Closure, with $x' = y' = z' = 0$ on the left and $x = y = z = 0$ on the right. Therefore $e = I_3$ is the identity element of $(\mathcal{G}, \cdot)$. $\blacksquare$
>
> **Inverses:** For all matrices in $\mathcal{G}$, we must find a matrix in $\mathcal{G}$ whose product with it equals $e$. Writing the parameters of the second matrix as $x', y', z'$ and requiring the product rule computed in Closure to give $\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$,
>
> $$x + x' = 0, \qquad y + y' = 0, \qquad z' + x y' + z = 0,$$
>
> which is solved uniquely by
>
> $$x' = -x, \qquad y' = -y, \qquad z' = -z - x y' = -z + x y.$$
>
> These parameters are again real, so the candidate
>
> $$\begin{bmatrix} 1 & -x & x y - z \\ 0 & 1 & -y \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \in \mathcal{G}$$
>
> lies in $\mathcal{G}$, and the product rule verifies it on both sides:
>
> $$\begin{bmatrix} 1 & x & z \\ 0 & 1 & y \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \cdot \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -x & x y - z \\ 0 & 1 & -y \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix},$$
>
> $$\begin{bmatrix} 1 & -x & x y - z \\ 0 & 1 & -y \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \cdot \begin{bmatrix} 1 & x & z \\ 0 & 1 & y \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix},$$
>
> since $(x y - z) + x(-y) + z = 0$ and $z + (-x)y + (x y - z) = 0$. Therefore every element of $(\mathcal{G}, \cdot)$ has an inverse in $\mathcal{G}$. $\blacksquare$

> [!error] (b) — $(\mathcal{G}, \cdot)$ is not Abelian
>
> **Counterexample:** Consider the two matrices in $\mathcal{G}$ with parameters $(x, y, z) = (1, 0, 0)$ and $(x', y', z') = (0, 1, 0)$, i.e.
>
> $$A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad B = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}.$$
>
> Using the product rule computed in Closure,
>
> $$A \cdot B = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad B \cdot A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix},$$
>
> which differ in the $(1,3)$ entry ($1$ versus $0$), so $A \cdot B \neq B \cdot A$. (In general the two products differ in their $(1,3)$ entries $x y' + z + z'$ and $x' y + z + z'$, which agree only when $x y' = x' y$.) Therefore $(\mathcal{G}, \cdot)$ is a group but not an Abelian group. $\blacksquare$
