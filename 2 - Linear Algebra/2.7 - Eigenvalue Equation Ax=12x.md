# Exercise 2.7 — Solving $\mathbf{Ax} = 12\mathbf{x}$

> [!question] Problem
> **2.7** Find all solutions in $\mathbf{x} = \begin{bmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \\ x_3 \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ of the equation system $\mathbf{Ax} = 12\mathbf{x}$,
>
> where
>
> $$\mathbf{A} = \begin{bmatrix} 6 & 4 & 3 \\ 6 & 0 & 9 \\ 0 & 8 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$$
>
> and $\sum_{i=1}^3 x_i = 1$.

## Solution

> [!success] — the unique solution is $x = \left(\tfrac{3}{8},\; \tfrac{3}{8},\; \tfrac{1}{4}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^3$
>
> The equation $\mathbf{Ax} = 12\mathbf{x}$ is equivalent to the homogeneous system $(A - 12I)x = 0$. The augmented matrix is
>
> $$[A - 12I \mid 0] = \left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} -6 & 4 & 3 & 0 \\ 6 & -12 & 9 & 0 \\ 0 & 8 & -12 & 0 \end{array}\right]$$
>
> We now apply elementary row operations, writing the operation for each step above its arrow, until we reach the reduced row echelon form:
>
> $$\xrightarrow{R_2 + R_1} \left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} -6 & 4 & 3 & 0 \\ 0 & -8 & 12 & 0 \\ 0 & 8 & -12 & 0 \end{array}\right]$$
>
> $$\xrightarrow{R_3 + R_2} \left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} -6 & 4 & 3 & 0 \\ 0 & -8 & 12 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{array}\right]$$
>
> $$\xrightarrow{-\tfrac{1}{6}R_1,\; -\tfrac{1}{8}R_2} \left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & -\tfrac{2}{3} & -\tfrac{1}{2} & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & -\tfrac{3}{2} & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{array}\right]$$
>
> $$\xrightarrow{R_1 + \tfrac{2}{3}R_2} \left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 0 & -\tfrac{3}{2} & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & -\tfrac{3}{2} & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{array}\right]$$
>
> The pivots lie in columns $1$ and $2$, so $x_3$ is free, and the reduced system reads $x_1 = \tfrac{3}{2}x_3$, $x_2 = \tfrac{3}{2}x_3$. Setting $x_3 = t$ with $t \in \mathbb{R}$ gives
>
> $$x = t \begin{pmatrix} \tfrac{3}{2} \\ \tfrac{3}{2} \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}.$$
>
> The normalization condition $\sum_{i=1}^{3} x_i = 1$ then yields $t\left(\tfrac{3}{2} + \tfrac{3}{2} + 1\right) = 4t = 1$, so $t = \tfrac{1}{4}$, and therefore
>
> $$x = \begin{pmatrix} \tfrac{3}{8} \\ \tfrac{3}{8} \\ \tfrac{1}{4} \end{pmatrix}.$$
>
> Indeed, $\mathbf{A}x = \left(\tfrac{9}{2},\; \tfrac{9}{2},\; 3\right)^{\mathsf{T}} = 12x$ and $x_1 + x_2 + x_3 = 1$. Since $\operatorname{rank}(A - 12I) = 2$, the eigenspace is one-dimensional, so the normalization selects exactly one vector. $\blacksquare$
